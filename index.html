How It Works

One executable on Windows. Full neural search in the cloud.

LeelaCloud runs locally on your Windows PC and connects directly to your own Google account. There are no developer-hosted proxy servers storing your chess games, credentials, or files.

Direct User-Owned Cloud Architecture

Register the exact same LeelaCloud.exe in ChessBase as a UCI engine and open it from Explorer as the desktop console. When analysis starts, LeelaCloud restores your verified Lc0 binary and neural network weights directly from your own Google Drive onto a fresh Google Colab GPU over an encrypted WebSocket SSH-2 channel.

Implemented Capabilities

Built for strength-first neural chess analysis.

Every component is designed around practical chess playing strength, low startup overhead, and transparent compute protection.

Google Colab GPU Support & Portable Multi-GPU Lc0

Prepare official Lc0 releases and recommended neural networks directly in the cloud — no local compiler, CUDA toolkit, or developer tools required. Engine builds target portable multi-GPU CUDA binaries with CUDA Graphs and CUTLASS support, paired with empirically measured strength-first optimization profiles.

  • Verified live operation on Google Colab NVIDIA T4 and L4 GPUs (with pre-flight support for compatible Colab tiers such as A100, G4, and H100 subject to user entitlement and regional capacity).
  • Neural networks prepare on Colab CPU runtimes and cache as single-copy verified uncompressed .pb weights for fast GPU startup.
  • Pre-allocation compatibility checks validate network min_version, VRAM fit, and activation safety guards before allocating paid compute.

Native ChessBase & UCI Integration

Automatically detects piped UCI launches from ChessBase and other UCI GUIs with zero console windows and millisecond registration latency — while double-clicking the same executable opens the desktop console.

  • Dynamic quick parameters for EngineVersion, Network, GPU, Threads, RamLimitMb, NNCacheSize, and MultiPV.
  • Live network and engine switching on an active GPU without reallocating your Colab runtime.
  • Instant ChessBase kibitzer unload while GPU teardown finishes cleanly in the background.

Lightweight Windows Architecture

Compiled in native Rust with Tauri 2 and Windows system libraries (Schannel TLS, Win32 Credential Manager, bundled SQLite). Zero local Python, Node.js, OpenSSH, or OpenSSL dependencies on the user's machine.

  • Single portable LeelaCloud.exe (~5.9 MiB) using standard Windows DLLs and built-in WebView2 Runtime.
  • Pure-Rust SSH-2 over TLS WebSocket with strict-kex, RFC 4253 rekeying, and host-key continuity pinning.

Compute Protection & Transparent Accounting

Opening the desktop console or registering the engine in ChessBase never allocates a GPU. Paid sessions require explicit user opt-in and are protected by layered safeguards.

  • Read-only 0-CU account GPU eligibility checks before requesting paid hardware.
  • Configurable idle shutdown, maximum session duration (with Windows sleep/hibernation detection and remote watchdog), minimum CU balance reserve floor, and optional soft session CU budget.
  • Durable local SQLite (WAL) history separating measured durations, provider-reported snapshots, and attributed estimates — with CSV and JSON export.
Privacy & Security

Your account, your Drive, your local vault.

LeelaCloud follows least-privilege access principles. All tokens stay in Windows Credential Manager on your own PC, and Drive access is strictly scoped to LeelaCloud-created cache files.

Narrow drive.file Storage Scope

LeelaCloud requests only Google's non-sensitive drive.file scope alongside basic profile identity (openid, userinfo.email) for Drive caching. It cannot list, read, modify, or delete your personal documents, photos, or unrelated Google Drive files.

Cryptographic Integrity & Instant Revocation

Every cached Lc0 binary and neural network restored from Google Drive is verified inline against locally pinned SHA-256 and BLAKE2b-256 chunk manifests before execution. Disconnecting an account deletes local OS vault credentials and sends an RFC 7009 token revocation request to Google.
